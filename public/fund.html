The Telescope

The broadcast that sets every group's agenda — worldwide.

Twice a week Douglas Gayeton and guests broadcast from the field. The first episode frames the week's challenge for groups. The second features what groups have done in response. Call and answer, week after week — the loop between local action and global signal.

Week 1 — How it begins
Step 1

Viewing Party

Group screens The Telescope #1: Nature 2026 — a global update from Douglas Gayeton and guests.

Step 2

Introductions

Say what matters to you. Orient yourself on the map. This is the first moment of the movement becoming real.

Step 3

Field Guide

Distribution of copies. Members read the introduction and agree on a local target to improve by saying Yes.

Step 4

Homework

Go to the website. Connect with other YES members. Find who else is working on what you care about.

Step 5

On the Map

The group appears on the YES Movement map. The movement becomes visible to itself for the first time.

Quarterly Summit

Where the movement
sees itself.

Every season ends with a Summit — groups converging in person to share what they've built, witness what others have done, and agree on what comes next.

The Summit is the movement's exhale. Community, synthesis, renewal.

First Summit

Water — Mississippi Headwaters

The first Summit gathers groups working on Water at the headwaters of the Mississippi River. A symbolic and geographic anchor for what the movement is building.

Who is behind it

Fifteen years of work. A network already in place.

The YES Movement is built on the work of Douglas Gayeton and The Lexicon — a California-based NGO that spent five years working with Google to develop an accelerator for good ideas, engaging over 1,000 organisations at the intersection of food, agriculture, conservation, and climate change.

The forthcoming book, Molotov Alva and the Museum of Extraction, is the intellectual and aesthetic foundation. It names the problem. The YES Movement builds the response.
